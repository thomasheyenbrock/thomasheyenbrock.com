How I think about Product

Have you ever worked on a feature that sounded right but ended up being a flop in production? Me too.

Have you ever been on a project that you believed in but ended up being cancelled for "business reasons"? Me too!

It sucks. And as Software Engineers we're often quick to assign the blame to others. "The product manager didn' think this through enough." Or, "Senior leadership does not see the value in what we're doing here."

But what really went wrong? TL;DR for me it always been a lack of de-risking the project. And Engineering always has a stake in that. Let me explain how I think about Product as a Software Engineer.

## The Risks

When I say "de-risk", then there must be some risks involved with a project. In fact, there are three questions that you should answer before deciding whether to do or not to do a project.

_Note: _

### Is it desirable?

Does the thing you're buildiung solve an actual user problem? This is non-negotiable, it has to be a "yes". And you need some form of proof for it: Direct user feedback, user research, usage data, or similar. Maybe you yourself can even feel the friction in your product.

Are you solving a hair-on-fire problem or a convenience-problem?

### Is it viable?

Does the thing help your company make money? Can you monetize it directly or could it be a sidecar that drives other areas of your product?

### Is it feasible?

Can you actually build the thing given your position and resources? How high is the complexity? How long to you estimate it will take you to implement?

Stellate: A startup-journey recap

I decided to join Stellate after the initial product launch in the Summer of 2021. I was in the first batch of people that got hired by the founders Max and Tim.

For the next almost three years I was part of a wild ride that ended with the acquisition of Stellate by Shopify and The Guild. I had the opportunity to work with some very smart folks and learned a ton. Time to bring it all to paper so I don't forget.

In this post I'm mostly summarizing and not get into a lot of details (which actually are way more interesting). I'm planning to blog more in 2025, so this gives me a good list of topics to write about in more detail...soon.

A place to belong

I'll start with the thing where Stellate knocked it out of the park: Its culture.

The founders set out with the company culture as one of their most important goals, and it showed in the result. The level of intentionality that permeated everything was unlike anything I've seen or heard of before.

Stellate showed me that Company Values can be so much more than buzzwords, and how to effectively use them to codify the culture that makes a company unique. More importantly, we lived those values in our day-to-day, which made everybody feel valued. Like being an essential part of something bigger. Like being in a place where they belong.

Stay nimble, embrace change

Stellate successfully launched the initial product and attracted many users for which the product solved a hair-on-fire problem. But where to go from there? How do you actually build a sustainable business model that powers growth? Essential questions for many startups I believe.

Stellate tried to stay nimble. When product-led-growth stalled, we tried expanding our product suite. When we didn't find product-market-fit with that, we re-focussed on our core product and tried attrackting bigger "enterprise" customers. When we realized the size of the market we played in was too small, we looked into ways of growing it.

Dealing with change can be frustrating, but it's an essential part at this early stage. I learned to reframe change as opportunity. In every new scenario there's new possibilities to shine, both for the individuals as well as the company as a whole.

Culture-add over culture-fit

Everybody in Stellate was unified in the same vision and mission. That didn't mean we always agreed. And that is good.

I've seen companies where people only hired others that mirror themselves, creating a culture where existing opinions and biases are reinforced. Going against the mainstream opinion is like an act of treason.

Stellate was not like this. We were very intentional about giving feedback and embracing a culture of different opinions. If we hired new people, then we were mindful of not just if they fit into our culture, but what they can add to it.

It wasn't perfect by any means. But we had awareness of the fact. And as they say that's often the most important first step that enables you to improve.

Working distributed around the globe

Stellate had no office. Everybody worked from home, whereever that was. At its height, our employees stretches from UTC+2 to UTC-8 timezones, meaning a whopping 10 hours of difference.

That setting brings its unique challenges. Collaborating well across that time difference required a lot of flexibility and intentionality. We created an async-first culture, with a delicate balance between meeting-time and time reserved for collaboration and connection. It was one of those things that constantly changed, where we always iterated on trying to make it fit our needs.

What else?

I'll be honest, above really feels like scratching the surface of what I learned over my time at Stellate. There's so many more concrete learnings that make up those points:

  • How to create a meeting culture in a distributed environment that fosters connection
  • How leadership is different from management, and how everyone can become a leader
  • How to make company values really work, not just as a marketing tool
  • How to do great job interviews and find the best people
  • How to grow as an individual and progress your career
